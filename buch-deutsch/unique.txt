50	[6]	Introduction All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds. I testify that there is no God worthy of worship except for Allah and that Mohammed (Peace and blessings upon him) is the final messenger of Allah.
51	[6]	This book is to learn Quran reading in the reading style
52	[6]	Hafs an Asim (Over the way of Ash-Shatibiyah)
53	[6]	which is often only called “Hafs”. The writing is like in the Medina edition. This Reading-Style and this print are the most common worldwide and the most common for non-Arabs to learn.
54	[6]	There are several common books to learn the reading of the noble Quran. Despite of this we at Abrar have decided to make a new one. The reasons for this are the following:
55	[6]	Many of those books are completely in Arabic. This makes it difficult for the foreign reader to
56	[6]	navigate through the book and even more difficult to understand the explanation of the rules. The reader often does not even know what the reading text is or what the explanation is.
57	[6]	Many of the common Qaida books have mistakes or are in illogical order.
58	[6]	The writing of many common Qaida books is not the writing of the Medina edition of the
59	[6]	Quran Book (Mushaf). This makes it difficult for the student to switch from the Qaida to the Quran.
60	[6]	The use of such books is often not free. This is why we make this book free of charge, and
61	[6]	the PDF may be used and shared freely. For those who like to have printed books, we plan to have printed copies for sale in sha Allah, so keep an eye out for those. You can print the book out for yourself or for your students if you wish.
62	[6]	For the student: You should take care not to write English letters in the book in order to speed up the reading and remember the pronunciation. Years of experience as teachers have shown us that students who do this are not making progress in reading and need twice as long as others to practise the same content.

96	[16]	Fatha
97	[16]	The Arabic language has 3 short vowels (Harakāt): Fatha (⟪AR⟫), Kasra (⟪AR⟫) and Dhamma (⟪AR⟫).
98	[16]	“Haraka” means “movement” because it shows how the lips are moved after the letter. Make sure you practise the movements correctly because it will make your recitation more correct and beautiful.
99	[16]	“Fatha” means “opening”. For the Fatha, you open your mouth wide. When the Fatha is written over the letter, it is pronounced with a short “a” at the end. This “a” is pronounced lightly (comparable to the word “hat”) if it is standing after a light letter and heavily (similar to the word “saw” in American English or the word “car” in British English) if it is following a heavy letter. Make sure that your tongue is relaxed and flat after the light letters and your tongue is raised to the roof of the mouth after the heavy letters.

132	[39]	A letter may come without Haraka. In this case⟪AR⟫ it always has a Sukoon (no matter if Sukoon is written or not). If a letter has Sukoon⟪AR⟫ we call it a “sakin (silent) letter”. 158	[47]	There are three lengthening Letters in the Arabic Language: Alif (⟪AR⟫), Ya (⟪AR⟫) and Waw (⟪AR⟫). They prolong the three different Harakat. Alif is used to prolong a Fatha. 161	[49]	Baby Madd Alif
162	[49]	Alif Madd can also be written as a “Baby-Madd”. Then it is just read exactly like a normal Alif Madd. The reason why the scholars wrote it like this is that it was not written but spoken in the original word. So the scholars added it in order for us to know that it is spoken with Alif.
163	[50]	Special ways to write Madd Alif
164	[50]	Sometimes Madd Alif is written like a “Ya” without dots. This is called “Alif Maksura”. It is spoken like a normal Alif Madd.
165	[50]	Sometimes a Baby Madd Alif is written over a “Ya” without dots or over a „Waw“. This shows that the original word was written with “Ya” but spoken with “Alif”. We follow the rule: When we find two letters on top of each other, we can always read the top letter! Just ignore the letter below and read a normal Madd Alif.

255	[88]	Lam Shamsiyah (The Sun-Lam) Half of the Alphabet was Moon letters. The other 14 letters are Sun-letters. How perfectly balanced is the Arabic language! All the Sun-Letters are very close to the Articulation point of the Lam. That means that two very close letters are meeting each other. This is why they melt together. The Lam becomes hidden (Idgham) and the first letter of the word becomes double (Shadda) because the Lam melted into it. This sentence is to memorize the Sun letters:

268	[92]	Iqlab
269	[92]	The second rule of Nun and Tanween is the Iqlab. Iqlab means “changing”. It means that we exchange the Nun with a Meem every time a Ba follows. In order to remember the rule, the Tanween and Sukun for Iqlab have a special writing in the Mushaf: They are written with a little Mim! When you read the Mim of Iqlab, it is read with Ghunna. That means we read it through the nose and make it long.

274	[93]	Idgham
275	[93]	The third rule of Nun and Tanween is the Idgham. It means “Melting”. The nun becomes merged into the next letter. Sometimes the Nun is completely gone, and sometimes the Nasality (Ghunna) of the Nun will stay. Take care: Again, the Tanween is written differently, or the Nun stands without any sign if this rule occurs.
276	[93]	When merging it into Mim or Nun, our Nun will be gone completely. Keep in mind to prolong the Ghunna of the Mim and Nun Mushaddada:
277	[93]	When merging it into Lam or Ra, our Nun is also merged completely. Lam and Ra are now read with Shadda. Make sure you don’t give them any Ghunna.
278	[93]	When merging it into Ya or Waw, our Nun is not completely merged. We do not read a Nun anymore, but now Ya and Waw get Ghunna and are prolonged. Because Ya and Waw don’t have any Ghunna from themselves this comes from the Nun.

280	[94]	Note: Idgham never happens within one word – only between two words.
281	[95]	Ikhfa
282	[95]	The fourth and last rule of Nun and Tanween is Ikhfa. Ikhfa means “hiding”. When pronouncing Nun in front of an Ikhfa letter, we do not touch the articulation point of the Nun with the tongue, but rather we go to the articulation point of the following letter. Note that the Tanween signs and the Nun without any Harakah for Ikhfa look the same as for Idgham.
